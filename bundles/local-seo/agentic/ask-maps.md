Ask Maps is Google's Gemini-powered conversational experience in Google Maps. A user taps the **Ask Maps** button, asks a complex place-based question, and receives a conversational answer with a customised map.

# Availability

Google announced Ask Maps on 12 March 2026, starting to roll out on Android and iOS in the United States and India, with desktop "coming soon."

**Current state, 16 November 2026.** Google said Ask Maps "starts expanding to 30 countries across Europe — including Spain, Germany, and the U.K. — bringing Ask Maps to more than 200 countries and territories globally"; the companion post gives the European figure as "30+ countries across Europe."[^google-ask-maps-holiday-features-2026-11-16][^google-ask-maps-europe-2026-11-16] The March announcement remains the record of the launch; it is no longer the current availability statement. Google publishes no per-country list, so treat the country-level answer as "ask Google's own page for that market" rather than as a list this document can assert.

Feature-level availability differs and should not be flattened into one answer:

* travel itineraries and saved-list search are "available everywhere Ask Maps is offered";
* "Know before you go" route tips and appointment finding are "launching first in the U.S., with more regions coming soon";
* Tell Maps contributions remain US-only, per the Help page quoted below.


# What it uses

Google says Ask Maps can draw on:

* information from more than 300 million places;
* reviews and other local knowledge from a community of more than 500 million contributors;
* places the user has searched for or saved in Maps; and
* route context, directions and estimated arrival times.

The experience can also help a user act on a result, such as making a restaurant reservation, saving a place, sharing it or starting navigation.

# What changes for a business

Ask Maps can synthesise constraints that do not fit neatly into a short keyword query: location, timing, route, atmosphere, availability and personal preferences. This increases the importance of complete, current and mutually consistent source data across the [Google Business Profile](/local-seo/gbp-signals.md), [reviews](/local-seo/reviews-reputation.md), menus, photos and first-party website.

Google has not published a separate set of Ask Maps ranking factors. Treat recommendations about profile completeness, media and review detail as data-quality practices, not guaranteed optimisation levers. See [Generative Engine Optimization](/agentic/generative-engine-optimization.md).

# Agentic appointment finding, November 2026

On 16 November 2026 Google added a capability that changes what the surface is. In its words, "with new agentic capabilities, you can choose to have Ask Maps find beauty, pet care, auto and home repair appointments online and call local businesses for you." A user asking "Find me a 4 p.m. nail appointment near me" is then shown "open slots across nearby businesses so it's easy to decide what to book."[^google-ask-maps-holiday-features-2026-11-16]

This is the first documented case of the assistant **acting on the phone line** rather than presenting a booking link. Google states none of the mechanics:

* no rule for which businesses are approached, or how a slot is identified as open;
* no list of supported booking or scheduling systems, and no eligibility criteria for a business to be called;
* no statement about whether a human, an automated caller or an agent places the call;
* no claim, in either direction, about ranking, visibility or conversion.

Those are unknown, not merely absent from this document. Do not infer a preparation requirement from them. The one operational point the capability does support is generic and already covered elsewhere: published hours, phone handling and stated service availability matter more when a third party is trying to reach the business on a customer's behalf.

The same release added three lower-stakes features — day-by-day **trip itineraries** with insider tips, numbered pins, live traffic and export to Google Docs on desktop; **saved-list search** ("Which spots saved in my lists have availability for 5 in 30 minutes?"); and "Know before you go" **route tips** covering parking, charging, weather, delays and tolls.[^google-ask-maps-holiday-features-2026-11-16]

# Tell Maps — contributing through a conversation

Google Maps Help documents a separate, named contribution experience inside Maps: **Tell Maps**. The Help page describes it as a way to "share your local knowledge through a Tell Maps conversation, an AI-powered contribution experience in Google Maps", and lists five contributor actions — report business hours, mark places closed, edit place attributes, share local tips, and upload photos. It also states the availability limit plainly: **"Important: You can only use this feature in the US."**

Two documented routes into it, both on Android phone or tablet:

* **From the Contribute tab** — open the Google Maps app, tap **Contribute**, tap the **"Tell Maps"** card, then type or speak a prompt.
* **Continued from an Ask Maps conversation** — when a contribution starts inside Ask Maps, it "automatically continue[s] the conversation in Tell Maps".

**Tips are not reviews.** The Help page draws the line explicitly: "Tips are short, bite-sized insights about a place, while reviews are a more comprehensive overview of a place that usually includes a star rating." Its examples are happy-hour deals, where the best seats are, and accepted forms of payment. Tips "may appear for others to see across Google, in places like the 'Know before you go' panel." Contributors manage and delete their own tips from their Maps public profile, and the page also documents updating a place from either surface.

For a business, this moves one part of the contribution surface from a form into a conversation and gives short-form tips a documented placement next to reviews. The page describes contributor actions only — it does not document an owner-side setting for tips, and it makes **no ranking or visibility claim in either direction**. The operational implication is the same one that applies to reviews and every other contributed field: the profile and the underlying data have to be accurate, because a conversational route makes it easier for anyone to suggest a change.
