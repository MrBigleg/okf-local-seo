Fake listings are profiles for businesses that don't exist as presented: invented names, addresses the operator doesn't occupy, and phone numbers that route elsewhere. They work because local search rewards apparent proximity and apparent reputation — both of which can be fabricated at scale for far less than the cost of being a real local business. The victims are consumers, and equally the legitimate businesses whose [visibility](/local-seo/gbp-signals.md) they displace.

# The tactic set

Documented in the 2026 US federal complaint described below, and consistent with what [profile auditing](/maps/gbp-profile-audit.md) and [review intelligence](/maps/review-intelligence.md) see in the wild:

* **Fabricated local identities** — fake business profiles created nationwide for local home services such as plumbing, heating or electrical work.
* **Address abuse** — listed addresses belonging to unrelated third parties, or made up entirely; nobody is at the location.
* **Phone-number laundering** — local phone numbers that route to overseas call centres.
* **Fake reviews** — fake consumer reviews praising the fake profiles, using images of people misappropriated from the staff directories of unrelated websites.
* **Subcontracted fulfilment** — when a customer does book, the work is passed to third-party contractors posing as employees, with no control over qualifications or quality.

# Enforcement is no longer only Google's

Google's own enforcement runs through [verification](/gbp/verification.md), content policy and suspensions. In 2026 listing fraud also became the subject of a federal civil complaint:

* In **May 2026**, the US Department of Justice — acting on an FTC referral, with the Illinois Attorney General — filed a civil complaint against an Illinois company trading as "Premium Home Service" (B.E.S.T. GDR, LLC) and its CEO. According to the DOJ's account of the complaint, the defendants created **over 15,000 fake business profiles on Google Search and Maps**, using addresses that often belonged to unrelated businesses and local phone numbers that routed to overseas call centres. (These are allegations, not adjudicated findings.)
* The complaint invokes the FTC Act, the FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (see [review gating](/gbp/review-gating.md)), the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, and two Illinois consumer-protection statutes.

# Why legitimacy signals matter

The enforcement story is also the optimisation story in reverse. Everything a fake listing must fabricate is something a real business can prove:

* **Verification** — video and live-call [verification](/gbp/verification.md) ask for proof a fabricated listing cannot easily supply.
* **Consistent NAP** — a real address and phone number that match across the web ([NAP & citations](/local-seo/nap-citations.md)) is expensive to fake per listing.
* **Organic review patterns** — steady velocity from accounts with history ([review intelligence](/maps/review-intelligence.md)), versus the burst patterns fake operations produce.
* **A real web footprint** — [local schema](/local-seo/local-schema.md), a matching website, and photos of a premises that exists.

For a legitimate business, the practical defence against fake competitors is to report them to Google and to be conspicuously verifiable yourself; for the profile-integrity side of the same problem, see [profile shielding](/gbp/profile-shielding.md).
