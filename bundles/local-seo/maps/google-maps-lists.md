Google Maps groups places into curated city lists. Google introduced them in March 2024 for select US and Canadian cities: a user searches for a city in the Maps app and swipes up to see them. Lists have since been observed in other countries, including the UK and Thailand. See the [Google reference](/references/google-maps-curated-lists.md).

# The list types

Google names three community lists, built from what people are interested in on Maps:

* **Trending** — updated weekly with places that recently saw a spike in popularity on Maps.
* **Top** — places the Maps community has consistently loved.
* **Gems** (shown as "Local Gems") — places that might be a neighbourhood's best-kept secret.

Publisher lists sit alongside them. Google's announcement names The Infatuation, Lonely Planet, The New York Times and OpenTable.

# What tracking shows (observational)

One first-party study tracked 14 lists daily for five weeks and compared them with captures from six months earlier ([reference](/references/ctb-maps-lists-research-2026.md), not yet verified). Its observations, which do not establish why Google includes a place:

* **Top Lists** nearly always cite two articles and barely change week to week, but about half of their places were replaced over six months.
* **Trending lists** are mostly new places: most opened in the last six months, few entries cite a source, and almost none of a list's places last more than a few weeks.
* **Local Gems** sit in between and lean on the business's own description and opening date.
* Cited sources are concentrated: Time Out and The Infatuation account for about half of all cited links in that sample.

# What a business can check

These are data-quality checks, not ranking levers.

* **Business description** — the "(From owner)" text on list entries appears to come from the profile's [business description](/gbp/business-description.md), so it may be read as the place's list blurb.
* **Opening date** — Trending and Local Gems entries show an opening date, which appears to come from the profile's opening date field.
* **Coverage** — Top List entries usually cite editorial roundups and reviews from the publishers that cover the area.
* **Re-check inclusion** — membership changes, so being on a list once says little about later.

Lists are one of the surfaces [Ask Maps](/agentic/ask-maps.md) users meet; how the two relate is not documented by Google.

# Open questions

* How Google selects, orders and removes places on each list type.
* Whether the "(From owner)" text and the opening date come from the Business Profile fields named above.
* Whether a place moves between list types as it ages.
