The Google Ads policy governing the **business name field in paid ads**. It is a different surface from the [Google Business Profile business name guidelines](/references/google-gbp-business-name-guidelines.md): this page governs what an advertiser may put in an *ad's* business name field, and it applies to ads formats that require a business name field.

# The standing rule

Google lists as not allowed:

> Providing a business name that's anything other than the domain, the recognized name of the advertiser, or the promoted downloadable app

with non-exhaustive examples of generic or location-based names ("Mountain View Plumber", "New York Taxi") and of promotional language ("Buy Acme Shoes", "Sale at Acme Home Services").

Two standing allowances sit alongside that prohibition:

* **Domain as business name** — the domain may be used with appropriate spacing if it fits the 25-character limit, e.g. `www.acmesolutions.com` → "Acme Solutions".
* **Resellers and authorized dealers** — these must use appropriate qualifying language; Google's example is a car dealer of Acme vehicles in San Antonio using "Acme of San Antonio".

# The October 2026 provision

Google's change notice states that "In October 2026, Google will update its Business name requirements policy to allow for differences between the business name and the destination domain in certain limited cases", provided that:

* The business name accurately reflects the recognized name or brand of the advertiser;
* There is a verified direct relationship between the advertiser and the domain owner; and
* The advertiser's products or services are directly offered in the destination domain.

The notice then states: "Third-party resellers, independent booking intermediaries, affiliate distributors, and secondary sellers do not qualify for this provision and are prohibited from using the standalone brand name of the goods, services, or properties they offer as their business name."

The policy page itself now carries the same three conditions under a heading titled **"Business name and destination domain differences"**, with the same exclusion list.

* The change notice is dated **"(Posted on October 1, 2026)"** and is written in future tense ("will update").
* As accessed **2026-10-25**, the policy page **does** contain the three-condition provision and the exclusion list.

# The notice is easy to miss

The change notice page is titled **"Update to Editorial Policy (October 2026)"**, while its body is entirely about the *Business name requirements* policy. A reader scanning the Google Ads changelog for a business-name entry will not find one under that name, and the policy it amends is a different page from the page carrying the announcement. Anyone tracking this change should watch the policy page itself rather than rely on changelog titles.

# Advertiser Verification interaction

Google adds a constraint that outranks the provision for verified advertisers:

> If you've completed Advertiser Verification, the business name must strictly match the legal name or the trade name submitted during verification. If your legal business name doesn't match or exceeds the 25-character limit, you can pursue Brand Verification if you own an active trademark registration for the business name you want to use.

So the three-condition allowance is available to advertisers who have not completed Advertiser Verification, or whose verified legal name already matches; a completed verification imposes a strict-match requirement instead.

# Enforcement posture

The policy page states that violations "won't lead to immediate account suspension without prior warning" and that "A warning will be issued at least 7 days prior to any suspension of your account." The documented remedies are editing the ad or asset (with automatic re-review, typically 24–48 hours) or appealing the policy decision.
