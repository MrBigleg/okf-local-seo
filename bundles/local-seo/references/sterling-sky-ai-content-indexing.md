A short Sterling Sky video (about four minutes, published 5 March 2025) describing one client who mass-published AI-written service area pages. It is a single anecdote from an agency, not a study. [^notebooklm-source-a9d80128]

> This page was written on 2026-11-16 from the video's captions and YouTube metadata, not from a viewing of the video. Captions can mis-transcribe names and numbers, and anything shown only on screen is not captured here. A human should confirm any figure against the video before relying on it.

# What the video reports

* A client published roughly 200 to 300 AI-generated service area pages at once, one per town in its state, against Sterling Sky's advice. [^notebooklm-source-a9d80128]
* Sterling Sky saw no visible increase in Google traffic after the launch. In Search Console there was no spike on the launch date. [^notebooklm-source-a9d80128]
* The stated reason: most of the pages were not in Google's index, so they could not rank. The only metric that rose was the count of non-indexed pages. [^notebooklm-source-a9d80128]
* Joy Hawkins' reading is that Google did not penalise the pages; it declined to index them because the content was not different enough from what it already had. [^notebooklm-source-a9d80128]
* She contrasts this with a separate case covered in another Sterling Sky video, recalled as about 3,000 pages, where Google issued a manual penalty. She calls that the worst case. [^notebooklm-source-a9d80128]
* Service area pages in general are described as still working for many Sterling Sky clients. Her objection is to doing them at scale, and she adds that she does not think Google really wants them. [^notebooklm-source-a9d80128]
* The closing line is that Google looks for quality whether or not the content is AI-generated. [^notebooklm-source-a9d80128]

# Limits

* One client, no site named, no page counts, index counts or dates given beyond what is listed above. [^notebooklm-source-a9d80128]
