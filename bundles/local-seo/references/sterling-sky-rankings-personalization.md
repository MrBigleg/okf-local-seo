# Why spot checks mislead, according to the video

* **Location.** A search made from inside the business sees the business ranked high because Google knows where the searcher is. A customer several miles away may be closer to competitors and see them first. The presenters say rankings are not universal and that small changes in location can reshape the results. [^notebooklm-source-f3b6b207]
* **Behaviour.** The presenters say Google states that not every result is personalised, and that search history, previous activity, device type and language settings can influence results. Their example: someone who visits their own website often may see it more. They add that the same applies to AI search results. [^notebooklm-source-f3b6b207]
* **Time and infrastructure.** They say Google is constantly indexing new content and that ranking changes do not reach every data centre at once, so two people with the same settings can still see different results. [^notebooklm-source-f3b6b207]
* **A visual cue.** Purple (visited) links in the results suggest the searcher's own history may be shaping what they see. [^notebooklm-source-f3b6b207]

# What Sterling Sky says it does instead

1. **Change the search location.** They use a Chrome extension called GS Location Changer to search from a city, postal code or address near the profile, or from the target town when checking a service area page. [^notebooklm-source-f3b6b207]
2. **Use a grid-based rank tracker**, which shows rank across an area instead of from one point. They name Local Falcon as their favourite and Places Scout and BrightLocal as alternatives. [^notebooklm-source-f3b6b207]
3. **Use an incognito window** where possible. [^notebooklm-source-f3b6b207]
