# The five tactics as presented

| Tactic | What the video claims | Evidence offered |
|---|---|---|
| Press releases | Sterling Sky had tested them years ago, seen no ranking effect and stopped. A recent retest for a realtor, using a release built on real data and linked to a landing page, was followed by an 83% traffic increase within 28 days and higher local rankings. [^notebooklm-source-9fd60681] | One client. No baseline, traffic source or dates given. [^notebooklm-source-9fd60681] |
| Business hours | The presenters call opening hours a ranking factor: rankings drop while a business is closed. They say a lawyer and a psychiatrist ranked lower when closed, and that Local Falcon data shows rankings shifting by the hour. They advise listing only hours when someone will answer the phone. [^notebooklm-source-9fd60681] | Two anecdotes and a reference to third-party data not shown in the captions. [^notebooklm-source-9fd60681] |
| "Near me" wording | They say adding phrases such as "near me" or "near you" can improve organic rankings, with less effect on the map pack, and that "near you" often works better. They report a LocalU audience split about evenly on whether it works. [^notebooklm-source-9fd60681] | "We have tested this a lot"; no test described. [^notebooklm-source-9fd60681] |
| Service area pages | They say these still work, citing a lawyer's page for Staten Island, but that building one for every city is less effective and can fail at scale. They recommend the business's own data, pricing, job photos, team and case studies. [^notebooklm-source-9fd60681] | One anecdote. [^notebooklm-source-9fd60681] |
| Long title tags | They say they ignore the 60-character guidance, that longer titles consistently perform better, and that one 229-character title "worked". [^notebooklm-source-9fd60681] | One example, outcome not quantified. [^notebooklm-source-9fd60681] |
