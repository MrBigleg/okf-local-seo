# Points that bear on local SEO

* **Reporting.** Quaid says a rankings PDF is data, not a story, and that clients who do not read it have not been told what matters. He wants agreed keywords and outcomes before the work starts and a periodic call. [^notebooklm-source-452e6d30]
* **Rank reports are getting weaker.** Hawkins says her team searched the same non-local keyword on the same morning and each saw a different results page, and that AI answers vary even more. Quaid's answer is to judge individual pages by whether they are cited and whether they lead to conversions. [^notebooklm-source-452e6d30]
* **Service area pages and cannibalisation.** Asked whether to build 50 location pages, Quaid's first concern is cannibalisation, including with the home page, and whether Google treats two places as the same. Hawkins disagrees for local: in her experience a service page can target the same term as the home page without harm, and the home page is the only exception she has seen. [^notebooklm-source-452e6d30]
* **Scaled content.** Hawkins says she has audited sites that lost large amounts of traffic after core updates, that she is tracking about twenty, and that beyond link patterns she now sees mass-published location pages as a common thread. She says the share of a site made up of such pages is a trend she has measured, and that she has seen no correlation with engagement metrics. Quaid speculates that publishing velocity, share of site and templated titles could be signals, and says a new site adding hundreds of pages at once is the obvious pattern. [^notebooklm-source-452e6d30]
* **Launching pages.** Quaid says his team launches many pages together but does not get them all indexed at once, holding back internal links on small sites. [^notebooklm-source-452e6d30]
* **Not indexed.** Hawkins notes that Google may not index content it already has many copies of, which she sees often in local. Quaid attributes "crawled, not indexed" to a lack of authority reaching the page and recommends internal links from pages that already get traffic. He advises no more than about ten body links from a home page. These are his rules of thumb. [^notebooklm-source-452e6d30]
* **Pruning.** Quaid says he does not favour blanket content pruning. In the project he describes, a call-centre site, his team unpublished about 30 pages that were competing with each other and reports traffic growing about 480%. He identifies competing pages as two URLs within a few positions of each other with similar impressions for the same query. [^notebooklm-source-452e6d30]
* **Diagnosing a traffic drop.** Compare like periods in Search Console; find whether one page, one phrase, branded traffic or the whole site fell; check for link changes at the same time. Quaid says most sites he sees get about 90% of traffic from about ten pages, and that he believes algorithmic penalties are rarer than people assume, with scaled content as an exception. Hawkins says that in local SEO she has seen more penalised sites in the past year than before. [^notebooklm-source-452e6d30]
* **Local links.** Quaid argues that a small number of links between real local businesses that recommend each other, such as a plumber and an electrician in the same town, are legitimate and useful. Hawkins agrees at low scale and warns that at scale it confuses what the site is. [^notebooklm-source-452e6d30]

# Points outside local SEO, recorded briefly

* Quaid describes exact-match domains, a position-17 rule for deciding when a query deserves a new page, and URL slug length as levers. [^notebooklm-source-452e6d30]
* He says he does not see AI assistants replacing Google for local intent, and builds a separate report for AI referral traffic. Figures he quotes for AI traffic shares and usage are from memory and unsourced in the interview. [^notebooklm-source-452e6d30]
