Whitespark's September 2026 study asks whether keywords inside the **business title field** of a Google Business Profile correlate with visibility in Google's top-3 local pack. The study is explicitly correlative: it reports the share of listings that earned top-3 visibility for each title variant and does not claim that renaming a business causes a ranking change.

# Method

Whitespark analysed **1.4+ million local business listings across 17 industries**, grouped each profile's title into one of three variants, and compared top-3 local pack visibility:

* **Exact-match** — the full keyword phrase appears in the title (Whitespark's example: a home-services brand named "Affordable Appliance Repair").
* **Partial-match** — part of the phrase appears (for example "Kelly's Appliance Center" containing "appliance" but not "appliance repair").
* **Zero-match** — no keywords in the title (for example "DG tech" in the appliance-repair category).

# Headline findings

* Keyword presence correlates with higher top-3 rates in most industries — Whitespark summarises the effect as "2x, 3x, and even 5x more top-3 rankings" depending on the industry — with exact-match usually outperforming partial-match.
* The spread is wide. In **travel & tourism** the exact-match rate was 29.54% against 6.05% for zero-match (the largest lift in the dataset); in **retail** 15.11% against 3.05%; in **beauty and personal care** 15.59% against 2.50%. **Real estate** was the weakest industry measured, at 3.92% exact-match against 2.31% zero-match.
* **Keyword position correlates too:** titles that begin with a keyword were associated with more top-3 rankings in 15 of the 16 industries with sufficient data.
* **Most titles are not optimised.** Zero-match titles vastly outnumbered both keyword variants in every industry, and partial-match titles outnumbered exact-match titles — exact-match titles were the least common variant in the dataset.

# Guidelines tension

Google's business-name guidelines require the title to reflect the business as it appears in the real world. Keyword stuffing therefore conflicts with the same policy this bundle records in [Google — business name guidelines](/references/google-gbp-business-name-guidelines.md). Whitespark's own recommendation is to re-brand or file a DBA rather than to stuff keywords, noting that a stuffed title can be reported by competitors or the public and, if corrected by Google, the ranking advantage disappears with it.

# AI-era limitation

Whitespark states it did **not** have access to AI ranking data, so it could not correlate optimised titles with AI-search prominence. Its AI comment is directional only — that Google retrieves GBP information in AI environments and that Business Profiles remain a rich local dataset. No causal or AI-visibility claim should be read into this study.
